The detection of natural disasters is very much crucial in minimizing the disastrous impact due to cyclones, floods, and severe storm conditions that result in the infrastructure and economic loss. The hyperspectral satellite imagery is a spatial-spectral monitor for disaster-prone regions; however, based on high dimensionality and spectral redundancy, one of the bottlenecks for computing is effective analysis. This research provides a novel Aerial Hyperspectral Imagery Classification framework, optimized preprocessing, tensor deconstruction, and deep learning-based the classification for disaster detection improvement. The preprocessing process includes Spectral Band Normalization by which it normalizes the pixel intensity distribution over spectral channels, and Outlier Removal by which it will help eliminate noise from data and enhance the quality. Tensor Compression through Higher Order Orthogonal Iteration (HOOI) can be applied to hyperspectral data where the dimensionality reduction is done but where important spatial-spectral features were preserved. Moreover, Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) is performed to enhance the quality of an image and to improve the visibility of spatial edges while keeping a balanced spectral intensity distribution. The 3D Convolutional Neural Network (3D CNN) optimized works for the Aerial Hyperspectral Image Classification in the classification phase. It works with multi-dimensional convolutions to extract complex three-dimensional spatial-spectral features, which compare their accuracy of classification with that of traditional methods and the associated decreasing computational costs by improvement of 5% and 30%, respectively establishing the position of the current proposed framework.
